# RFC 0031: Wi-Fi

- **Status:** Draft
- **Author:** Mateusz Pianka
- **Created:** 2026-11-05
- **Last changed:** 2026-11-05
- **Supersedes / superseded by:** —

## Summary

Wi-Fi owns the Wi-Fi radio. It scans, keeps the networks the device's
owner has joined, joins one of them on its own, and tells the rest of the
system the link's state. It runs in a new **network** program, which
later also holds the Network service of
[RFC 0015](0015-network.md). Passphrases are secrets in Settings
([RFC 0025](0025-settings.md)), so Settings learns to keep secrets first.

## Problem

[RFC 0007](0007-services.md) lists Wi-Fi among the services (scanning,
known networks, connecting) and puts it in the radios program, but no RFC
says what it does. The Network service (RFC 0015) needs a link to carry
apps' traffic; Time wants NTP over it
([RFC 0028](0028-time.md)); adb over the network and firmware updates
come over it ([RFC 0012](0012-developer-access.md),
[RFC 0013](0013-firmware-updates.md)).

Lessons from the system before this one, on the same hardware:

- After a failed join, scans and joins kept failing until the radio was
  taken down and up again.
- Once the radio runs, the kernel heap has about 14 KB of 160 KB left,
  16 KB with mobile data up too after trimming the radio's buffers
  (measured on the T-Deck Max in September 2026;
  [current/architecture-v2.md](../current/architecture-v2.md)): the
  radio's buffers are the tight resource.
- A network's passphrase belongs to that network: an old one must never
  be tried on a new network.

## Proposal

### What others see

The interface, in the system interface of [RFC 0005](0005-communication.md):

| Call | Does |
|---|---|
| **Scan** | a stream of the networks in reach ([RFC 0004](0004-layers.md) streams): name, signal, security, channel; ends when the scan is over |
| **Known** | the networks joined before, last used first, without their passphrases |
| **Join** | a network by its name, with a passphrase for a new one, or a new passphrase that replaces a known one's; it becomes known once joined |
| **Forget** | a known network, and its passphrase |
| **Leave** | the network joined, until the owner joins one again or Wi-Fi is turned off and on |

A topic, **`wifi`**, says the state: off, on and not joined, joining,
joined; the network's name, its signal, the device's address, and why the
last join failed (a wrong passphrase, no answer, no address). Turning
Wi-Fi on and off is a setting of its own (`wifi.enabled`), off until the
owner turns it on.

The system UI and the developer's shell may call it (a command typed in
the shell has the system UI's rights, as in RFC 0025), and Location
later, for positioning from the networks in reach. Apps reach the network
through RFC 0015's Network service, never Wi-Fi.

### Known networks and passphrases

- **A known network** is its name, its security and, for a secured one,
  its passphrase: an open network and a secured one of the same name are
  two. They are Wi-Fi's settings: RFC 0025's schema is fixed and has no
  lists, so Wi-Fi keeps **16 slots,** each three settings, `net.<n>` the
  name, `sec.<n>` the security and `psk.<n>` the passphrase. The slots
  are kept last used first, rewritten at each join in one gathered write
  (RFC 0025); joining a seventeenth network forgets the one used longest
  ago.
- **A network's name** is up to 32 bytes, which need not be UTF-8: one
  that is not is kept and shown with each such byte escaped (`\xNN`).
- **The passphrase is a secret** (RFC 0025): encrypted at rest, never
  logged, shown as dots, read only by Wi-Fi, through a request to
  Settings (the service library has none yet). Each network keeps its
  own; joining a new network never tries another's.
- **Securities:** open networks, WPA2-Personal and WPA3-Personal. WPA3
  needs SAE in NuttX's Wi-Fi driver, to be checked on the device.
  Enterprise networks (802.1X) wait for a later change.

### Secrets before Security

RFC 0025 encrypts secrets under a key Security derives from the device
key ([RFC 0014](0014-security.md)). Security is not built yet, and the
device key's fuse is burned only once, for good. Until then:

- **Settings keeps a random key of its own,** from the chip's random
  number generator, in a file beside its settings, and encrypts each
  secret under it with authenticated encryption (AES-GCM, a fresh nonce
  at each write).
- **It is weak, and said to be:** in the flat build
  ([RFC 0002](0002-build-mode.md)) any native code can read the file, and
  anyone who reads the flash over USB has the key and every secret.
  Settings logs a warning at each start while it runs so, and the system
  UI shows it under Developer options.
- **Factory reset** removes the key with the settings it opens.
- **Once Security derives the key,** Settings encrypts every secret again
  under it, and deletes its own key.

### Joining

- **On its own:** when Wi-Fi is on and no network is joined, it scans and
  joins a known network in reach: the one joined last first, then the
  strongest.
- **A network that fails** is tried less and less often, up to every
  8 minutes; others in reach are tried meanwhile. A wrong passphrase is
  not tried again until the owner gives another.
- **After a failed join,** Wi-Fi takes the radio down and up before the
  next scan or join (the lesson above).
- **A lost network** is joined again the same way.
- **The address** comes by DHCP, and names are looked up by the servers
  DHCP gives, through NuttX's network stack. The Network service
  (RFC 0015) takes over names and the choice of link once it is built.

### The radio and power

- **Off until turned on,** and off again when the owner turns it off; the
  radio is down and its memory given back.
- **While joined,** the radio's own power saving: it sleeps between the
  access point's beacons.
- **Sleep:** whether a scan or a join holds the device awake (RFC 0004
  asks a service to say so), and whether the device may light-sleep while
  joined, is measured with the second step; the Wi-Fi driver shares the
  sleep clock's calibration ([RFC 0028](0028-time.md)).

### How the program runs

- **A new program, `network`,** holds Wi-Fi now, and RFC 0015's Network
  service later. RFC 0007's programs change: the radios program keeps
  Radio, Bluetooth and NFC; the network program holds Wi-Fi and Network.
  A fault in a packet radio's or Bluetooth's setup no longer takes the
  device's network down, and the other way round.
- **One event loop** (RFC 0004): a scan or a join is a request to NuttX's
  Wi-Fi driver and a wait for its event, not a blocked thread.
- **It starts after the system program,** for Board's description, its
  settings and Service states. A device without Wi-Fi runs the network
  program without it (RFC 0007); Board's description
  ([RFC 0024](0024-board.md)) gains the Wi-Fi radio with the second step.

### Not in this RFC

Enterprise networks, hidden networks (which a scan does not show), a
hotspot of the device's own, Wi-Fi Direct, networks that ask for a login
page first (captive portals), positioning
from Wi-Fi networks (Location's, through this interface), and choosing
between Wi-Fi and mobile data (RFC 0015's Network service).

### The order of the work

1. **Secrets in Settings,** with the key of its own.
2. **The network program and Wi-Fi:** on and off, scan, join, the topic,
   known networks, joining on its own.
3. **NTP for Time,** over whatever link is up (RFC 0028's order: the
   modem's network, then NTP, then GNSS). Until Network is built, Time
   watches the `wifi` topic rather than Network's, and looks the server's
   name up itself through the servers DHCP gave; RFC 0028 is amended
   then.

Each step is a pull request of its own, tested on the phone.

## Rules it follows

- **One owner.** Wi-Fi alone drives the Wi-Fi radio (RFC 0004).
- **Secrets stay secret:** passphrases never leave Settings and Wi-Fi, and
  are never logged.
- **Optional.** A device without Wi-Fi breaks nothing: Time keeps its
  other sources.
- **Topics carry nothing private:** a network's name is shown to the
  owner, but no passphrase goes anywhere.

## Alternatives

- **Wi-Fi in the system program,** beside Board and Power. One program
  fewer, but a fault in the radio's driver would take Settings and Time
  down with it.
- **Wi-Fi in the radios program,** as RFC 0007 had it. It shares a
  program with LoRa and Bluetooth, which fail in ways of their own.
- **Passphrases in a file of Wi-Fi's own,** encrypted. No wait for
  secrets in Settings, but a second place for secrets, and RFC 0025
  already names passphrases among Settings' secrets.
- **The device key's fuse burned now.** The strong key at once, but a fuse
  cannot be unburned, and the key's derivation is Security's work, not
  built yet.

## Costs and risks

- **Memory:** the radio's buffers come out of the kernel heap, already
  tight; NuttX's options for them are kept small, and measured.
- **The weak key** until Security: a copy of the flash gives every
  passphrase away. Said so in the log and the UI.
- **One more program:** its stacks and its loop cost RAM.

## Open questions

- When to burn the device key's fuse, and with it build Security's key
  derivation.
