# RFC 0028: Time

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Author:** Mateusz Pianka
- **Created:** 2026-10-23
- **Last changed:** 2026-10-29
- **Supersedes / superseded by:** —

## Summary

Time keeps the wall clock right and the time zone known. It takes the time
from the modem's network, from NTP or from GNSS, in that order of
preference. Between those readings it corrects for the error of the clock
that times light sleep, which it learns from them. The zone is a named one
the owner picks, or by default the network's offset. Wake timers and
alarms, also Time's ([RFC 0007](0007-services.md)), come in a later RFC.

## Problem

- **The clock gains while the chip sleeps.** Light sleep is timed by an
  internal RC oscillator that runs faster asleep than when it was
  calibrated. On the T-Deck Max the clock gained 107 ppm of the time
  slept, about 9 s for a day asleep, with the sleeps of at most 2 s the
  board's poll for USB power allows (2026-10-29); its rate wandered by
  some tens of parts per million within the hour, and earlier firmware
  gained 132 ppm. Single sleeps of about 24 s had gained about 0.85 %
  (2026-10-17); why they differ is not known. The board has no 32 kHz
  crystal to time sleep instead.
- **Nothing sets the clock.** After a power loss it starts from a fixed
  date in the kernel's configuration (6 December 2011 today). The previous
  system ([current architecture](../current/architecture-v2.md)) set it
  from the modem's network time, or by NTP, every 6 hours, which still
  left the error to build up between settings, and none of it corrected
  offline.
- **There is no time zone.** Programs show UTC.

RFC 0007 makes Time the one keeper of the wall clock, the time zone,
network time and the timers that wake the device, as
[RFC 0004](0004-layers.md) asks. This RFC covers the clock and the zone.

## Proposal

### Where the time comes from

Time takes the best reading available. When several are at hand within
their uncertainty, it prefers them in this order:

| Source | From | When |
|---|---|---|
| **The modem's network** | Telephony, which reads the modem's clock, set by the network (`AT+CTZU=1`), with the network's offset and summer time | when the modem is registered and the network has sent the time; not every network does. Costs no data |
| **NTP** | Time itself, over the network | when Wi-Fi or mobile data is up already: Time never brings a connection up for the time |
| **GNSS** | Location | when the receiver has a fix, while something else uses it |
| **The owner** | the system UI's clock setting | when none of these has spoken |

- **Sources publish their readings,** each with its uncertainty and
  when it was taken (by the monotonic clock), on the topics
  `network_time` (Telephony) and `gnss_time` (Location), which Time
  reads. Information comes up as events (RFC 0004), and a topic needs no
  check of who calls. A topic gives a new reader its last message,
  however old: Time adds a reading's age to it, and leaves one older than
  a minute aside.
- **Time reads NTP itself** when Network announces a connection that is
  up ([RFC 0015](0015-network.md)), from the server in its setting
  `ntp_server` (`pool.ntp.org` by default), whose address it asks of
  Network, which RFC 0015 gives the name lookups.
- **A reading is taken at most hourly,** at the device's next wake after
  the hour while a source is cheap to reach (the modem registered, a
  connection up); nothing wakes the device for the time. Reading the
  modem's clock keeps the chip awake about a second.

### Between readings: the sleep clock's rate

The crystal times the chip while it is awake, so only the time it sleeps
goes wrong, and by a fairly steady factor.

- **Time learns the factor.** At each reading it compares the clock's
  error with the time slept since the last reading, which NuttX's power
  management counts (`/proc/pm`), and keeps a smoothed estimate of the
  sleep clock's rate.
- **Until it has learnt it, the factor is the device's:** the sleep
  clock's rate measured on that kind of device, a part of the device's
  description ([RFC 0024](0024-board.md)). Time asks Board for it once
  at start. A device whose description gives none goes uncorrected until
  Time has learnt its rate. So far Time does not learn: that needs a
  source of the time, and none is written yet.
- **The kernel applies it** where it turns a light sleep's length into
  clock time (the Espressif layer of the NuttX fork), so the clock, the
  monotonic clock and the timers all come out right. Power owns the
  decision to sleep (RFC 0007): Time gives Power the factor, and Power
  gives it to the kernel.
- **How it travels:** Time announces the factor on a topic, `sleep_rate`,
  in parts per billion of the time slept, marked learnt or the device's;
  Power hands it to the kernel with `boardctl(BOARDIOC_SLEEP_RATE)`.
  After each light sleep the kernel takes the rate's share of the time
  slept off the system timer. The same clock times the wake-up, so the
  sleep asked for is lengthened by the rate less 50 ppm: it ends just
  before the next timer, and not late unless the rate given is more than
  50 ppm above the true one. On the T-Deck Max, with its measured rate as
  the device's, the clock gained about 1 ppm of the time slept, give or
  take 3, over 44 minutes asleep: 2 ms at the end (2026-10-29).
- **The factor is kept** in `/var/lib/time`, with the last good time,
  written with each reading (at most hourly, a few bytes) and as the
  program stops. A restart starts corrected, and a restart after a power
  loss starts near the right time, marked as not yet confirmed, rather
  than in 2011. Across a restart without a power loss, the chip's own RTC
  keeps counting, so the clock needs no file then.
- **The owner's own time is a fallback,** replaced by the next reading
  from a source; there is no "automatic time" switch.

### The time zone

- **Time's settings** (owner `time`, [RFC 0025](0025-settings.md)):
  `zone`, an IANA zone name (`Europe/Warsaw`) that the system UI picks
  from the list of zones; `zone.auto`, on by default; and `ntp_server`.
- **With `zone.auto` on, the zone follows the network:** the offset and
  summer time the modem reports. A network-given offset has no rules for
  the future, but the network announces each change. Until a network has
  given an offset, and on a device without a modem, the named zone
  applies, with its rules.
- **With it off, the named zone applies,** with its rules. The build
  compiles every zone of the tz database it is built with into its POSIX
  rule (`CET-1CEST,M3.5.0,M10.5.0/3`): 9 to 13 KB, depending on whether
  old names (links) are kept. The table is part of the service library,
  read-only data in the image like `/usr/share`'s
  ([RFC 0010](0010-file-layout.md)); it is generated from the tz
  database by a tool and kept in the source, so every build has the same
  rules. Old rules are not kept: dates long past may show an offset that
  was different then.
- **Programs and apps convert times themselves,** in any zone by name,
  with the service library's rules, and the runtime gives apps the same
  ([RFC 0008](0008-runtime.md)): a calendar needs many zones at once,
  where NuttX's `localtime()` takes one for the whole system
  (`LIBC_LOCALTIME` stays off). They learn of the device's zone from the
  `time` topic.

### What others see

- **The `time` topic** ([RFC 0005](0005-communication.md)) announces a
  change of the zone, and a step of the clock with its size, so that
  programs showing the time, or holding timers by the wall clock, redraw
  or re-arm.
- **The `pnut.Time` interface:** `Get` gives the time, the zone and its
  rule, the source of the last reading and when it was taken; `Set` sets
  the owner's own time. Callers are known by program
  ([RFC 0006](0006-manager.md)): only the `ui` program may `Set`.

Time is a module of the system program (RFC 0007).

## Rules it follows

- **One owner** of the clock (RFC 0004): nothing else sets it; others only
  report.
- **Readings come with events** (the modem registering, a connection
  coming up), and at most hourly while such a source is up, taken when the
  device wakes anyway: nothing wakes the device for the time. This bends
  RFC 0004's rule against polling as RFC 0007 does for the battery: a
  reading an hour costs about a second awake.
- **No connection for the time alone,** so the time costs neither data nor
  a radio's power.

## Alternatives

- **Re-setting the clock every few hours, without the rate.** Simpler, but
  the error builds up between settings, seconds in a few hours at the
  rate measured, and without a source the clock just runs fast.
- **NTP only.** One mechanism, but offline (most of the time) the clock
  drifts, and the modem's free network time goes unused.
- **A 32 kHz crystal.** The cure, but the board has none; the next device
  should.
- **Correcting with `adjtime()`.** The ESP32-S3 port does not support it,
  and it would correct only the wall clock, not the timers that sleep
  delays too.
- **Only an offset setting.** Smallest, but the owner would change it
  twice a year.
- **`zone.auto` from the network's country** (its MCC) and offset, as the
  previous design had: it gives the zone's rules, so summer time comes
  right offline. Following the offset was chosen instead; this stays an
  option for later.
- **The whole tz database, with its history.** Exact for old dates, but
  hundreds of kilobytes.
- **A `Report` method that sources call.** Time would check the caller's
  program, but the sources would depend on Time's interface, against RFC
  0004's events going up.
- **NuttX's `localtime()` with one zone for the system.** Nothing to
  write, but a calendar showing another zone's times could not.

## Costs and risks

- **9 to 13 KB** of zone rules in the image; a module in the system
  program.
- **Patches in the fork** to apply the factor: a `boardctl()` command,
  and the Espressif layer's correction. The sleep's length comes from the
  slow clock's calibration in Espressif's HAL, which the Wi-Fi and
  Bluetooth drivers also read: scaling it there would move the radios'
  sleep timing too, so the calibration is left alone, and the factor is
  applied after it, to the clock and to the length of the sleep asked
  for.
- **The learnt factor is only as good as the readings:** the RC
  oscillator's error changes with temperature. Smoothing, and readings
  every hour when they are free, keep it close; how close the learnt one
  comes is to be measured when Time learns it.
- **A network may give a wrong time,** and a false base station may give
  one on purpose. A reading far from the clock as corrected (more than a
  few minutes, plus the drift since the last reading) is held until a
  reading from another source agrees with it; the same source agreeing
  with itself proves nothing. The first reading after a power loss, or
  after the owner set the time, is taken as it is: with one source only,
  setting the time by hand is how the owner lets its readings in again.
- **Telephony and Location do not exist yet:** until they do, and NTP is
  written, the time comes from the owner, and the device's rate keeps it.

## Open questions

- **How large a step** the clock takes at once, rather than catching up
  through the rate: to be measured with the first sources.
